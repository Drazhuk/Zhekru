# Zhekru

> A black feather weighs the silence inside an antlered dragon's mask.

## The Scale in the Empty Chapel

The chapel stands at the crossing of roads that lead to different villages by day and to the same hill after dark. Its bell tower is empty. Its altar holds a balance made of pale wood, with a black feather resting on one side.

The other side contains nothing visible. Nevertheless, it hangs lower.

A caretaker visits at noon to polish the balance. She uses a cloth embroidered with antlers. After each cleaning, the embroidery changes its direction, though the cloth remains folded in precisely the same way.

Visitors leave small objects beneath the altar: buttons, broken rings, little stones worn smooth by water. None is placed on the balance. The caretaker says the empty pan already carries everything it can bear.

## A Pilgrim With a Wooden Mask

A pilgrim arrived carrying a mask carved from dark wood. It had no eye openings. Across its forehead ran the outline of branching horns, and inside its mouth lay a narrow strip of white cloth.

He had found it hanging from a tree on the hill where the roads met at night. When he took it down, the tree's shadow remained above him, spread across a sky without clouds.

The caretaker set the mask beneath the balance. The black feather bent toward it. From the empty pan came the sound of somebody drawing a curtain across a window.

The pilgrim remembered that sound from a room he had visited only once. He remembered waiting beside a bed and looking at the curtain because he had been asked not to look elsewhere.

He could no longer remember who had made the request.

## The Pan That Held a Hill

The caretaker unfolded her embroidered cloth. In its center appeared a small hill surrounded by roads. At the summit stood the same tree, now carrying a curtain instead of a mask.

The pilgrim placed one of the altar stones beside it. The hill became larger within the cloth. A doorway appeared among the roots, and beyond the doorway lay a room whose window faced the chapel from an impossible direction.

He recognized the bed. On it rested an antlered shape covered in a white sheet. Beside it sat a person holding a black feather against a bowl of clear water.

The balance trembled. Its empty pan rose slightly, and the pilgrim felt something shift beneath his ribs, as if a weight he had mistaken for part of his own body had changed its place.

## The Mask's Sleeping Owner

The chapel had been built where a dragon once laid its mask upon the earth. The hill was the curve of its brow. The tree had grown from an antler, and the roads followed the lines along its wooden face.

The creature slept beneath the bed in the remembered room. Its white covering was a field seen from below. Its breathing moved the chapel's balance, carrying silence from one pan to the other until a visitor recognized what the emptiness held.

The caretaker had learned this from the embroidered cloth. Before her first visit, its antlers had been ordinary flowers. Afterward, she found herself able to polish only the balance, never the objects left beneath it.

The pilgrim lifted the white strip from the mask's mouth. It smelled faintly of water left overnight beside an open window.

## The Feather's Narrow Writing

Along the black feather's shaft appeared four marks:

*Zhe — kru — el — voth.*

He traced them with the white cloth. In the embroidered room, the person beside the bed lowered their feather into the bowl. The water darkened, then cleared, showing the pilgrim standing beneath the tree with both hands empty.

He remembered leaving the room. He had closed the curtain himself, not because he had been asked, but because the light had begun to hurt the sleeper's eyes.

The mask acquired two narrow openings. Through them he saw the hill in ordinary daylight. The balance became level, and the empty pan released a sound too quiet to identify as either a breath or a word.

The caretaker folded her cloth. Its embroidered antlers now surrounded a little blank circle.

## The Roads After Dusk

The pilgrim left the mask on the altar. Its dark wood had become pale, and the white strip rested beneath its chin like a small folded towel. He carried away nothing that could be weighed.

At the crossing, he waited for night. The roads remained separate. He followed the one toward his village, noticing that the hill's tree stood farther away than he remembered and cast a shadow in the proper direction.

The caretaker still visits the chapel at noon. The balance still holds its black feather. Beneath the altar, the little stones have begun to acquire pale rings, one around each place where a hand once rested.

The bell tower remains empty.

But on evenings when the roads nearly agree, something beneath the hill opens its eyes behind a wooden mask, and the chapel's curtain moves once in a room that has never had a window.
